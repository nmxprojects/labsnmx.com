DesignRebrand · US19 November 2026

Docker drops the box-carrying whale for “Run on Trust”

Docker unveiled a new identity on 19 November: a simplified whale with a containment symbol, a new signature blue (#6172FF) and a new line, “Run on Trust”. The pitch moves from shipping containers to running software and AI agents safely.

NMX take: When your product’s job changes, the logo has to stop describing the old job. The whale stays, the boxes go, and that’s exactly the right amount of change.

DesignRebrand · US19 November 2026

Docker drops the box-carrying whale for “Run on Trust”

Docker unveiled a new identity on 19 November: a simplified whale with a containment symbol, a new signature blue (#6172FF) and a new line, “Run on Trust”. The pitch moves from shipping containers to running software and AI agents safely.

NMX take: When your product’s job changes, the logo has to stop describing the old job. The whale stays, the boxes go, and that’s exactly the right amount of change.

Source: DEV Community (Docker) · Our deep dive

DesignIdentity · US19 November 2026

Maker’s Mark gets a new identity, dipped in red wax

Turner Duckworth has redesigned the identity of the Kentucky bourbon brand, built around the hand-dipped red wax seal that has been on its bottles for decades.

NMX take: Own one thing so completely that it becomes the brand. Maker’s Mark wax is bourbon’s Tiffany blue, and the redesign is smart enough to lean in.

Source: Creative Boom

DesignStrategy · UK18 November 2026

FutureBrand says the “finished brand” is over

Relaunching itself, FutureBrand argues that static identity systems can’t keep up now that brands appear everywhere and more of their output is made by AI. Its answer is designing “brand behaviour”: how a brand looks, moves, talks and sounds, and how an AI trained on it makes decisions. The report, From Artefact to Infinite, came out on 18 November.

NMX take: Half of this is agency positioning, half is true. If an AI is going to make your ads, your guidelines need rules for judgement, not just logo clear-space.

Source: Design Week · Our deep dive

DesignRebrand · UK11 November 2026

Koto gives Giffgaff a signpost-led rebrand

UK mobile network Giffgaff has a new identity from London studio Koto, built around the idea of signposts pointing customers to their next chapter.

NMX take: Telecom branding is usually tariffs in a trench coat. A wayfinding idea at least gives the brand something to say besides price.

Source: Creative Boom

DesignOpinion · UK3 November 2026

Koto: every electric car brand looks the same

Branding studio Koto published a piece arguing that electric car brands have converged on one look: minimal wordmarks, soft gradients and the same calm, techy tone.

NMX take: Blanding has reached the car park. If your category all looks alike, the cheapest advantage left is a personality.

Source: Creative Boom

DesignStudio · UK2 November 2026

How&How becomes HOW+HOW, with a website built for AI search

The London and LA studio renamed itself HOW+HOW after six years, with a volume-dial logo, a new strategy and a site designed to be read by AI search as well as people.

NMX take: When a branding studio designs its own site for AI readers, take the hint. Being quotable by machines is now part of the brief.

Source: Creative Boom

AIAdobe · US10 November 2026

Adobe MAX 2026: your AI assistant can now use Photoshop

At MAX in Miami Beach (10–12 November), Adobe spread its Creative Agent across Creative Cloud, put Firefly Design and Firefly Graph into public beta, and previewed local MCP connections that let outside agents such as ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini edit native Photoshop, Illustrator, Premiere and After Effects files.

NMX take: The interesting bit isn’t another generator. It’s Adobe admitting your favourite AI might not be Adobe’s, and building the door anyway.

Source: Digital Trends · Our deep dive

AITools · Global2 November 2026

Affinity finally comes to iPad, free

Canva’s Affinity, the free pro app for vector, photo and layout work, has arrived on iPad, giving illustrators a free all-in-one option on the tablet.

NMX take: The cheapest serious design kit on the market just got portable. Freelancers paying for three subscriptions should do some maths this weekend.

Source: Creative Boom

AIFigma · India15 October 2026

Figma brings code layers and Config to India

At its biggest international Config yet, in India on 15 October, Figma moved code layers into open beta, so teams can build code-backed prototypes on the canvas, and announced an education programme and an MCP partnership with Sarvam.

NMX take: Design files are turning into working software. Learn enough code to read what the canvas now produces.

Source: Figma Newsroom · Our deep dive

AIBusiness · GlobalAugust 2026

Canva lowers its growth forecast as AI costs bite

Canva cut its expected annual revenue growth from 30% to 20% in August 2026, a slowdown reported alongside the rising cost of the AI features it offers.

NMX take: Even the design-for-everyone giant is finding AI expensive to give away. Expect more credit limits and paid tiers across every tool you use.

Source: Wikipedia, citing Fortune

AIAI design · Global2026

Figma pushes into marketing assets

Alongside Make (prompt to prototype) and Sites, Figma launched Buzz for producing on-brand marketing assets at scale from designer-controlled templates.

NMX take: If your brand system lives in Figma, Buzz may replace a lot of Canva work. If it lives in Canva, stay put.

Source: Spanbox · Our deep dive

AIAI design · GlobalOctober 2025

Canva relaunches Affinity as a free pro app

Canva relaunched Affinity as a single free desktop app for vector, photo and layout work; its AI features need a paid Canva plan.

NMX take: The best free alternative to Creative Cloud for logos, print and packaging. Budget for AI credits separately.

Source: UX Magic · Our deep dive

Advertising, search and platform changes, with what to do about each.

MarketingLaunch · UK19 November 2026

GTA VI lands, and so does an influencer kit with bite

Grand Theft Auto VI launched on 19 November. Birmingham agency Fluid marked it with “Xbox Has More Bite”, an influencer kit sent to 80 creators across eight countries.

NMX take: For the biggest launch of the year, the smart play wasn’t a billboard. It was 80 parcels designed to be unboxed on camera.

Source: Design Week

MarketingSearch · Global10 November 2026

Google starts its November 2026 core update

Google began rolling out its third broad core update of 2026 on 10 November, for all sites and languages. It says the rollout may take up to two weeks, and advises waiting until it finishes before reading Search Console data.

NMX take: Rankings will wobble right before Black Friday. Don’t rewrite your site mid-rollout; measure a full week after it ends.

Source: Search Engine Journal

MarketingAdvertising · Global5 November 2026

Christmas ad season opens with stop-motion and cartoons

Early standouts in the 2026 festive ad season include a handcrafted stop-motion film from Levi’s and Prada’s cartoon wonderland directed by former Disney animator John Pomeroy.

NMX take: Craft is the new flex. In a feed full of AI-smooth video, visibly handmade work gets noticed.

Source: Creative Bloq

MarketingAdvertising · UK3 November 2026

John Lewis’s Christmas ad: hated online, loved by the testers

Gloria and Pickles, the 2026 John Lewis Christmas ad starring Dame Kristin Scott Thomas, drew a fierce online backlash, with YouTube comments switched off. Yet System1 scored it 5.6 stars, the retailer’s best festive result since Excitable Edgar in 2019.

NMX take: Twitter is not your customer. The loudest reaction and the most effective ad are often different things, and this year proves it.

Source: Creative Bloq · Our deep dive

MarketingSearch · Global8 October 2026

Google’s September spam update finishes after 14 days

Google’s September 2026 spam update completed around 8 October after nearly two weeks, the longest spam rollout of the year.

NMX take: Spam updates hit scaled, thin and copied content. If your blog is mostly reworded AI summaries, this is your warning shot.

Source: Optimixed

MarketingQuick commerce · IndiaJuly 2026

Blinkit’s lead keeps widening

Blinkit handled about 331 million orders in the April–June 2026 quarter and ended it with 2,443 stores, nearly three times Instamart’s order volume.

NMX take: Scale is turning into range, and range into habit. Challengers now need a reason to be opened that isn’t speed.

Source: Analysis Atlas · Our deep dive

MarketingQuick commerce · IndiaJuly 2026

Flipkart Minutes passes 1,000 fulfilment centres

Flipkart’s quick-commerce arm crossed 1,000 micro-fulfilment centres across 130+ cities, with a target of about 1,500 by the end of 2026.

NMX take: Expect electronics and non-grocery categories to be where Flipkart picks its fights.

Source: Brand Chanakya (industry statistics) · Our deep dive

MarketingQuick commerce · IndiaJune 2026

Zepto’s IPO paperwork moves ahead

Zepto reportedly received SEBI’s go-ahead and filed an updated draft prospectus, which also published rare side-by-side order data for Blinkit, Zepto and Instamart.

NMX take: Prospectuses are the best free market research in India. Read the peer tables, not the headlines.

Source: Brand Chanakya (industry statistics) · Our deep dive

MarketingBrand stories · IndiaFebruary 2026

Blinkit’s founder now runs Eternal

Deepinder Goyal stepped down as Eternal group CEO from 1 February 2026; Blinkit founder Albinder Dhindsa took over, with Goyal staying on as vice chairman.

NMX take: The parent brand was renamed for this moment: the biggest business now leads a group named after none of its apps.

Source: Business Standard · Our deep dive
