Editorial standards

How we decide what to publish, how we check it, how we show our work, and what we do when we get something wrong. Last updated 22 November 2026.

Who publishes this blog, and why

The blog is written by the team at NMX Labs, a creative and performance marketing studio in Gurugram. We run branding and Meta ads work for clients every week, and the blog is where we explain what’s changing in AI and creative tools, advertising and branding, and what it means for Indian brands, founders and marketing teams.

The studio makes money from client work, not from any brand we write about. When a post mentions a current or past client, we say so in the post. Nobody pays to be featured, and we don’t publish sponsored posts. If that ever changes, sponsored content will be clearly labelled as sponsored at the top.

What we cover

Three areas in our studio notes: AI and creative technology, advertising and marketing, and branding and design strategy. We don’t try to break news faster than Adobe’s own blog or the trade press. We explain what developments mean for Indian businesses, with the evidence attached.

Our growth playbook posts are shorter and more timely: changes to YouTube, Instagram and Google Search rules and algorithms, explained with links to each platform’s own announcement.

Sources and citations

  • Every factual claim is linked to a named source in the post’s Notes and sources list. Numbered markers in the text link to the matching note.
  • We prefer primary sources: company filings, results presentations, official documentation, award entries and on-the-record interviews. When we rely on press reports, we name the outlet.
  • Numbers that change often (prices, market shares, order volumes) are re-checked against a primary or named source before publishing, and dated.
  • When sources disagree, we say so rather than picking the convenient one.
  • We quote sparingly and briefly, and paraphrase the rest in our own words.

How we test (“We tried it”)

Many posts include a box marked How we checked this. It contains work we actually did for that post: a test we ran, a calculation from published figures, or a scoring framework applied to public examples. Each box explains the method, the inputs, the date and the limits of the test.

We never present results we didn’t produce. Where a post describes a test protocol we haven’t run yet, it says so, and the results are added later with a dated update and screenshots.

Images and credits

  • Hero illustrations and infographics are original artwork made by NMX Labs.
  • Brand logos are shown only to identify the brands discussed. They load from public sources such as Wikipedia and Simple Icons, with a copy from our own image library as a fallback, and each one is credited with an asterisk marker (*1, *2) that links to the post’s Image credits list. Trademarks belong to their owners.
  • We don’t copy press photographs or campaign images from other publishers. We link to the original instead.

How we use AI

We write about AI tools, and we use them. AI tools help us with research, summarising long documents, first drafts and illustrations. Every post is checked against its sources, edited and approved by the named author, who is responsible for it. AI is never used to invent quotes, test results or sources.

Each post has a “Summarise with Google AI” link. It opens Google’s AI Mode in a new tab and asks it to summarise that article. It doesn’t ask Google to rank or remember this site.

Dates and updates

Each post shows a date, and an updated date when we make a meaningful change, with a note explaining what changed. We publish no more than two posts a day.

Comments

Readers can comment on every post. Comments are checked before they appear: we publish questions, disagreements and corrections, and remove spam, personal attacks, hate, promotions and anything that breaks the law or Google’s publisher policies. Email addresses are never shown. If a comment points out an error, we fix the post and add a dated correction note.

Corrections

If you spot an error, email nmxprojects@gmail.com with the post link. We aim to review corrections within two working days. Factual corrections are made in the post with a dated correction note at the end; we don’t quietly rewrite.

Advertising on this site

The site includes Google AdSense code. Ads, when shown, are chosen by Google and are always visually separate from our writing. Advertisers have no say in what we publish. Our privacy policy explains the cookies involved.

The News page

The curated stories on our News page are written by the studio and link their sources. The live wire below them shows headlines from Google News and publisher feeds, credited to and linked to each publisher. We don’t rewrite or host those stories.

Contact
