We’ve all read the recipe blog with a 2,000-word life story before the ingredients. Answer-first writing is the opposite, and it’s simply polite. It also happens to be exactly what AI tools and featured snippets look for.

The shape of an answer-first page

  1. The answer. One or two sentences that would satisfy someone who reads nothing else.
  2. Key takeaways. Three or four bullets with the important specifics: numbers, conditions, exceptions.
  3. The detail. Sections that explain why, how and what if, each with a clear heading.
  4. Quick answers. A short FAQ for the follow-up questions.

Before and after

Before: “Branding is one of the most important investments a business can make. In today’s competitive landscape, there are many factors to consider when thinking about…”

After: “A starter brand identity from a small studio typically costs a few hundred to a few thousand dollars, depending on what’s included. The biggest cost drivers are research, the number of concepts and the size of the guidelines.”

The second one can be quoted. The first one can only be skipped.

Headings people actually ask

Write headings as the questions people type or say: “How long does a rebrand take?” rather than “Timelines”. They help skimmers, they help search engines, and they give AI tools a neat question-and-answer pair to work with.

Format for the job

  • Comparisons: a table. Machines and humans both read tables brilliantly.
  • Steps: a numbered list.
  • Definitions: one plain sentence, then an example.
  • Everything else: short paragraphs, one idea each.

Answer-first doesn’t mean boring. Keep your voice. Just stop making people dig for the thing they came for.
