Every brand deck has a page of beautiful colour swatches. Most brands then use all of them, everywhere, at random. The result looks like a paint shop exploded. The fix isn’t fewer colours you like; it’s colours with jobs.

Give every colour a job

  • Main colour: what people remember you by. Used often, but not for everything.
  • Action colour: buttons, links, the thing you want tapped. Ideally used for nothing else.
  • Neutrals: backgrounds and text. These do most of the work and get none of the credit.
  • A rare accent: for moments: a launch, a sale, a sticker. Rare is the point.

Write it like a rule: “Orange is only for buttons and one highlight per page.” A rule like that is something a junior designer, a developer and an AI tool can all follow.

Check the contrast

Pretty pale grey text on a white background is how brands quietly lose older readers and anyone on a phone in sunlight. The WCAG accessibility guidelines ask for a contrast ratio of at least 4.5 to 1 for normal body text. Free contrast checkers tell you in seconds. Check every text and background pair you plan to use.

Type: two families, one scale

Most brands need a display face with character for headlines and a very readable face for everything else. Sometimes one family does both. Then set a simple scale (for example 14, 16, 20, 28, 40, 56) and stop inventing new sizes for every page.

Check the boring things too: does the font have the characters you need, like ₹, €, £ or other scripts? Is the licence fine for apps, ads and your website? Those two questions save expensive surprises.

Write the values down

Hex codes, RGB, font names, weights and sizes, on one page. Not as a picture inside a PDF, but as text people can copy. That page becomes the source of truth for your website, your ads and every AI tool you ask to “make it on brand”.
