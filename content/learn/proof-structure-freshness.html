AI tools are cautious readers. They’re trying not to say something wrong, so they lean on pages that look like they know what they’re talking about. Here’s how to look like that, mostly by actually being that.

Proof

  • Named authors. A real person with a role and a profile page beats “Admin”.
  • Linked sources. Every number gets a source. It helps readers check you and helps machines trust you.
  • First-hand evidence. Something you tested, measured or did. “We ran 20 product photos through three tools” is evidence no one else has.

Structure

Clear headings, short sections and visible dates help everyone. Behind the scenes, schema markup (small bits of structured data in your page code) tells search engines what a page is: an Article with an author and a date, an FAQPage with questions and answers, an Organization with a name and a logo. Most site builders and SEO plugins can add it for you.

Some sites also publish an llms.txt file: a plain-text map of the site for AI tools. It’s a proposed convention, not a guaranteed ranking factor, but it’s cheap to add and it does no harm.

Freshness

An answer engine doesn’t want to quote last year’s prices. Review your most important pages on a schedule, update the facts, and show a “last checked” date. Don’t just change the date without changing anything; people and machines both notice.

Measure what you can

There’s no perfect report for AI visibility yet, so do it by hand. Once a month, ask ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google’s AI Mode the ten questions your customers ask, and note which brands and pages they mention. Track branded searches in Search Console too. When more people search for you by name, something is working.
