Why brands are signing up for cricket beyond the biggest matches

The DP World Asia Cup Rising Stars 2026 in Doha has brought another brand into cricket’s commercial frame: Excitel, now the tournament’s Global Sponsor. Its placements go beyond a logo on a boundary board, reaching the pitch mat and the backdrops used for presentations and interviews. The deal is a useful look at why brands keep investing in cricket, and what turns sponsorship visibility into something people may actually remember.

The current Asia Cup has a different kind of brand story

There is more than one Asia Cup story in 2026. The DP World Asia Cup Rising Stars is being played in Doha from 19 to 29 November. Eight teams are competing across 15 matches, with India A, Pakistan A, Nepal and the UAE in Group B. The semi-finals are scheduled for 26 and 27 November, followed by the final on 29 November.2

The commercial picture is specific. DP World appears in the tournament name, while Excitel has been announced as its Global Sponsor. Excitel’s branding is scheduled for the pitch mat behind the wicket, perimeter boards and the backdrops used for post-match presentations, press conferences and player interviews. Matches are being shown on Sony Sports Network and Sony LIV. Those are broadcast platforms, not sponsor signings.1

What Excitel is buying with the deal

A cricket sponsorship is not one placement. It is a sequence of opportunities to be seen in different moments of the same match:

  1. The pitch mat sits close to the action, placing the brand within the camera’s match coverage rather than only at the edge of the ground.
  2. Perimeter boards create repeated exposure as the camera follows play, while presentation and interview backdrops carry the logo into post-match coverage.1
  3. The combination matters: the brand can appear during play and again when the match is being discussed afterwards.

Why brands keep signing up for cricket

Cricket gives advertisers something that is harder to manufacture in a conventional campaign: a reason for people to keep looking at the same environment over several days. A fan may skip an ad, but the fan still watches the next over, the next wicket and the next presentation. A sponsor can earn repeated visibility without needing every appearance to behave like a direct-response ad.

The value is bigger than a logo

For marketers, the strongest cricket sponsorships usually connect four things:

  1. Consistency. The brand appears across multiple match moments, not just one isolated burst.
  2. Context. The setting gives the brand a useful association, such as speed, performance, teamwork or shared viewing.
  3. Content. Sponsorship assets can become material for social posts, short-form video, interviews and match-day conversations, provided the relevant rights are in place.
  4. A next step. A viewer gets a reason to search, visit, try, subscribe or remember the brand after the match.

Attention is expensive. Context is what makes it work

Cricket advertising can become costly around the biggest fixtures. As a historical reference, The Economic Times reported that the 2025 Asia Cup India-Pakistan television spot rate was ₹14–16 lakh for 10 seconds, while a TV co-presenting package was priced at ₹18 crore. Digital co-presenting and highlights packages were reported at ₹30 crore. These were 2025 rates for that edition, not the price list for the 2026 Rising Stars tournament. The useful lesson is not to copy the old rate card; it is to recognise how sharply inventory value can change with the match and platform.3

What a smart brand team should ask before signing

Before buying a cricket sponsorship, a marketing team should be able to answer three questions:

  1. Which audience and business objective are we paying to reach: awareness, consideration, trial or sales?
  2. Which exact assets are included, where will they appear, and how often will viewers realistically see them?
  3. What will we run around the sponsorship: a useful offer, a repeatable content format, a creator collaboration or a clear post-match action?

The smaller brand does not necessarily need the largest logo. It needs the clearest connection between the audience, the product and the moment. A broadband company can build a story around the way fans watch. A food brand can own the match-night ritual. A payments brand can make a simple fan action easier. Those are strategic examples, not claims about the current tournament’s sponsor inventory.

Quick answers

Which brands are sponsoring the Asia Cup Rising Stars 2026?

DP World appears in the tournament title, and Excitel has been announced as the Global Sponsor. Sony Sports Network and Sony LIV are the broadcast platforms for the matches; they should not be confused with the confirmed sponsor list.1

What does a cricket sponsorship give a brand?

It can provide visibility in the stadium, during live coverage and around post-match media moments, depending on the contract. The strongest deals connect those placements to a campaign idea and a measurable next action, rather than treating logo exposure as the finish line.

The bottom line

The Asia Cup is a reminder that cricket sponsorship is not reserved for brands chasing the biggest celebrity or the loudest final. The Rising Stars edition gives a brand a different story to tell: backing the next generation while being present in a tournament that fans can follow match by match. Excitel has secured the visibility. The harder part, for any sponsor, is turning that visibility into a memory, a preference and eventually a customer. In cricket, the logo gets on the field. The brand still has to do the work.
