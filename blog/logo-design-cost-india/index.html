How much does a logo cost in India, and what are you actually paying for?

Quotes for “a logo” range from a few thousand rupees to several lakhs. The difference is rarely the drawing. It’s everything that happens before and after it.

By Anisha, NMX Labs · Market Research Lead27 August 20264 min read
Summarise with Google AI (opens Google AI Mode in a new tab)

Quick summary

  • Logo prices differ mainly in research, number of directions, testing and the files you receive, not in drawing time.
  • Cheap logos often skip a trademark check and leave you without source files.
  • Ask what’s included: discovery, directions, revisions, a logo system, guidelines and IP assignment.
  • Whatever you pay, insist on owning editable vector files and written IP assignment.

Summary by Anisha. Prefer an AI take? Ask Google AI to summarise it (opens in a new tab).

“How much for a logo?” is the question we’re asked most, and the honest answer is a question back: what do you need it to do? A logo for a weekend market stall and a logo that will sit on packaging, a website, an app icon and a shop sign for ten years are different jobs, even if both end up as a small mark.

Here’s how the market in India roughly breaks down, based on the quotes our clients bring us and our own pricing. These are typical ranges, not rules.

The price levels, roughly

WhoTypical rangeWhat you usually get
Template sites and contest platforms₹2,000–₹10,000A mark chosen from many quick submissions, little or no research, limited file types
Freelance designers₹10,000–₹50,000One designer, a few concepts, revisions, a set of files. Quality varies widely
Small studios₹25,000–₹2,00,000Discovery, several directions, a logo system with colour and type, short guidelines
Branding agencies₹3,00,000 and upStrategy, naming, research, full identity system, packaging and rollout support

Our own starter identity system begins at ₹25,000. We mention it so you can weigh this post knowing where we sit.

Where the money actually goes

Drawing a clean mark takes a skilled designer hours, not weeks. The rest of a good logo project is:

  • Discovery. Understanding the business, the customer and the competitors well enough to know what the mark must not look like.
  • Exploration. Several genuinely different directions, not one idea in five colours.
  • A pre-filing check. A basic look at existing marks in your classes, so you don’t build a brand on a name or symbol someone else already owns. A professional search and filing are separate, and we cover them in our branding checklist.
  • Testing at real sizes. Does it hold up as a 32-pixel favicon, a WhatsApp profile picture, an embossed bottle cap, a black-and-white invoice?
  • The system. Horizontal and stacked versions, a symbol, colour codes for screen and print, typefaces and clear-space rules.
  • Files and handover. Vector masters, exports for everyday use, and the rights.

When a quote is very low, one or more of those steps is missing. Often it’s the last one.

Red flags in a cheap logo

  • Only a PNG or JPG delivered. You can’t print it large, put it on packaging or edit it later.
  • A mark that looks familiar. Stock icons and lightly edited templates are common at the bottom of the market, and they’re hard or impossible to protect.
  • Fonts with no licence. A typeface used in a logo needs a licence that covers commercial use.
  • No contract. Without written assignment, the designer may still own the copyright in the work.

Questions to ask before you pay

  1. How many distinct directions will I see, and how many rounds of revisions are included?
  2. Will you check for obviously similar existing marks?
  3. Which versions and colour variants are included?
  4. Which file formats will I receive, including the editable source?
  5. Do I get short guidelines on how to use it?
  6. Will the copyright be assigned to my company in writing?
  7. Are fonts licensed for commercial use, and who buys the licence?

The files you must own at the end

  • Vector masters: SVG, PDF and the editable source (AI or similar).
  • PNG exports with transparent backgrounds, in light and dark versions.
  • A favicon or app-icon version of the symbol.
  • Colour codes in HEX, RGB and CMYK, plus Pantone if you print packaging.
  • A signed document assigning the intellectual property to your company.

““mark” includes a device, brand, heading, label, ticket, name, signature, word…”

Trade Marks Act, 1999, section 2(1)(m), via Indian Kanoon

So what should you spend?

Spend in proportion to how hard the logo has to work. If you’re testing an idea with a few hundred customers, a modest, clean wordmark in a good typeface is fine, and you can invest later. If you’re about to print 20,000 boxes, raise money or open a shop, the logo is the one asset that touches everything, and the cost of redoing it later (new packaging, signage, listings, confused customers) is much higher than doing it properly now.

Whatever you choose, the files and the rights matter more than the price. A ₹15,000 logo you fully own is worth more than a ₹1,50,000 logo you can’t edit.
