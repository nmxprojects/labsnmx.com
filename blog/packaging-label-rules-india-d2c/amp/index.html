Packaging labels in India: the mandatory details your designer must leave room for

A beautiful pack that’s missing the unit sale price or the month of manufacture can be held up at a warehouse or a marketplace. Plan the legal text before the artwork.

By Anisha, NMX Labs1 October 20263 min read
Listen to this articleSummarise with Google AI
Quick summary
  • Packs sold at retail must carry the maker or packer, generic name, net quantity, month and year of manufacture, MRP, unit sale price and consumer care details.
  • Online listings must show the same mandatory declarations as the physical pack.
  • Food products also need FSSAI labelling, and some categories have their own rules.
  • Lock the legal panel with your compliance adviser before the creative design starts.

This is a designer’s overview, not legal advice. Labelling rules are amended regularly and differ by product category. Check the current rules on the Department of Consumer Affairs website and with a compliance professional before you print.

Packaging is where branding meets regulation. Founders tend to brief the front of the pack and forget that a good part of the back and sides is already spoken for. When the legal text arrives late, it gets squeezed into whatever space is left, at the smallest size that fits, and the design suffers. Here’s what to plan for from day one.

The mandatory declarations

The Legal Metrology (Packaged Commodities) Rules, 2011 set out what pre-packaged goods sold at retail must declare. In broad terms:

“Unit Sale Price for all pre packed commodities meant for retail sale is introduced”

Press Information Bureau, Ministry of Consumer Affairs release
  • Name and address of the manufacturer, packer or importer (and for imports, the country of origin).
  • Common or generic name of the product, so a buyer knows what it is beyond the brand name.
  • Net quantity in standard metric units, or by number where that applies.
  • Month and year of manufacture.
  • Maximum retail price, inclusive of all taxes.
  • Unit sale price, such as price per gram or per millilitre, so buyers can compare pack sizes.
  • Best before or use by date for products that become unfit for consumption.
  • Consumer care details: a phone number and email address for complaints.

Declaring both the unit sale price and the month and year of manufacture became mandatory on all pre-packaged commodities from 1 January 2024, which caught out a lot of older pack designs. The rules also set minimum heights for the numerals of the net quantity and price depending on the size of the principal display panel, so check the table in the rules rather than guessing.

Online listings count too

If you sell through your website or a marketplace, the rules require e-commerce sellers to display the mandatory declarations on the product page as well. In practice that means your listing needs the same information as the box. Build it into your product page template once, rather than relying on a photo of the back of the pack.

Category rules on top

  • Food and beverages also follow FSSAI labelling rules: the FSSAI logo and licence number, the vegetarian or non-vegetarian mark, ingredients, nutrition information, allergens and more.
  • Cosmetics have their own labelling requirements under drugs and cosmetics law, including ingredient lists.
  • Medical devices now follow the Medical Devices Rules for these declarations, after a 2025 amendment moved them out of the packaged commodities rules.

Products that sit near category boundaries, such as nutraceuticals or medicated skincare, are where professional advice matters most.

Designing a pack that fits all of it

  1. Write the legal panel first. Get the final declarations from your compliance adviser before the creative design starts, in every language you’ll print.
  2. Give it a real home. Design a dedicated information panel with a clear hierarchy, not leftover space around a barcode.
  3. Plan for variable data. Manufacturing month, batch number and sometimes MRP change between runs. Leave a defined, uncoated area for printing or stickering them.
  4. Check legibility at real size. Print a proof at 100% and read it at arm’s length in ordinary light. Thin type on a metallic or busy background fails here.
  5. Design every size. A 30 ml tester has a tiny panel and the same obligations. Design it first; scaling up is easy, scaling down is not.
  6. Mirror it online. Use the approved legal text for your listing so the pack and the product page never disagree.

Common mistakes we fix

  • MRP shown without “inclusive of all taxes” wording.
  • Unit sale price missing on smaller packs.
  • Consumer care details pointing to a founder’s personal number that nobody answers.
  • Combo and gift packs designed without their own declarations.
  • Festive sleeves that cover the information panel of the pack underneath.

Packaging is one of the most expensive things a brand prints, and reprints are where budgets disappear. Fold the legal panel into your brand guidelines so every new SKU starts from an approved layout.
