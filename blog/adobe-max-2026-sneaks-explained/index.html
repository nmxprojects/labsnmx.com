Adobe MAX 2026 Sneaks: all 10 projects, ranked by how useful they are for brand work

Sneaks are Adobe’s research previews, and not all of them ship. We went through all ten from this year and sorted them by what they’d actually change for a brand or ad team.

By the NMX Labs team21 November 20265 min read

Every year at MAX, Adobe researchers go on stage and show experiments that might one day become features. That session is called Sneaks, and it’s usually the most fun part of the conference. This year actor Terry Crews co-hosted, and ten projects were shown across video, audio, imaging and design.

Two things were different in 2026. First, almost none of the projects rely on typing a prompt. They’re built around brushing, dragging and pointing, which is a quiet admission from Adobe’s own researchers that chat alone isn’t precise enough for visual work. Second, for the first time you can try some of them straight away: Project Deep Brush and Project Hands On are open in the new Adobe Labs Playground, along with a preview of a Photoshop capability called Project Custom Filters.

A Sneak is not a product announcement. Adobe makes no promise that any of these will ship, and if they do, the name and behaviour may change. That said, Sneaks have a decent track record: features like Photoshop’s Relight started life on the Sneaks stage.

Here are all ten, grouped by how much they’d matter to a team making brand and campaign creative.

Would change our week

Project Rinse Repeat

Copy and paste, but for edits. You do a piece of retouching or adjustment on one area of an image, then paste that edit onto other areas, where it adapts to each target. Adobe’s demo applied a retouch on one face in a group photo to everyone else’s face, fitted to each person. Crucially, every edit is rebuilt with real Photoshop tools, layers and masks, so the result stays non-destructive and you can keep refining by hand.

For brand work: this is the one we’d want first. Product catalogues, team photos, a grid of SKUs that all need the same shadow and colour correction. It also got one of the loudest reactions in the room, largely because it isn’t generative in the usual sense.

Project Deep Brush (try it now)

Deep Brush uses AI to understand the shape, colour and lighting of an image, separating it into physically meaningful layers. Brush strokes then follow the contours of a surface, colours can change while shadows stay natural, and you can paint light directly into a scene. Demos included adding artwork to a wall and a tattoo to a portrait so that both sat convincingly on the surface.

For brand work: mockups. Putting a logo on a curved bottle, a print on a fabric fold, a mural on a textured wall. Today that’s careful displacement maps and patience. It’s open in Adobe Labs, so it’s worth an afternoon.

Project Hands On (try it now)

Hands On recognises what’s in an image and puts handles directly on the canvas, so you can move or reshape things by dragging instead of describing them. The demo stretched a sofa and repositioned a person’s arm, with a live preview before the image model renders the change.

For brand work: layout fixes on generated or stock imagery, such as moving a product so it doesn’t sit under the headline, or making space for a CTA in a 9:16 crop.

Project Link Up

An illustration tool that reads your intent from where you drag an element and what you drop it next to, so connected parts of a design rearrange sensibly. Coverage described it as making vector design playful again.

For brand work: infographics, icon systems and diagram-heavy carousels, which are a big share of LinkedIn and Instagram educational content.

Useful for video-led brands

Project Scene Sweep

Scene Sweep understands the characters, locations and scenes across a whole video sequence, so one creative decision can be applied consistently across cuts and camera angles. Change a jacket colour once and it holds across every shot.

For brand work: fixing continuity in a shoot after the fact, or making a version of an ad film with a different product colour without reshooting.

Project On Cue

Controls exactly when things happen in generated video, using timing curves you draw or extract from existing footage.

For brand work: generated video that lands on the beat. Today most AI video ignores your edit’s rhythm.

Project Live Set

Lets you direct a video while it plays: change the subject, the environment or the camera angle as the scene continues.

For brand work: rapid concept exploration for ad films before you spend on a real shoot.

Project Sound Bender

The one audio project. You can shape a sound in real time, match its movement to a video, or start from a description and build something new, adjusting it on a canvas rather than only through text.

For brand work: sonic logos and short stings for reels, where getting a sound to match on-screen motion currently takes a sound designer.

Interesting, less immediate

Project Sight Span

Turns a single image into a navigable 3D preview you can move a camera through, then renders a new high-resolution image or a camera-controlled video from the angle you pick. Adobe’s demo stepped inside Vermeer’s The Milkmaid. It invents what wasn’t in the original frame, so it’s a reframing tool, not a record of reality.

For brand work: extra angles from a single hero shot for set design and world-building. Be careful using it on real products or locations where accuracy matters.

Project Screen Test

Builds a fictional character’s look, voice and personality, then lets you talk to the character and see how they’d react in different scenes as animated screen tests.

For brand work: developing a brand mascot or recurring character before committing to illustration or animation budgets.

The pattern worth noticing

Line the ten up and a direction shows. Adobe isn’t trying to replace the designer with a text box. It’s trying to remove the repetitive parts of the job (Rinse Repeat, Scene Sweep) and give AI better controls than words (Hands On, On Cue, Deep Brush). That fits what we see on client work: the bottleneck is rarely generating an image, it’s making the fifteenth adjustment precisely.

If you only do one thing, open the Adobe Labs Playground and try Deep Brush on a product shot. Then read our take on the main MAX 2026 announcements, which are the features you can actually plan around this quarter.

Sources: Adobe’s Sneaks announcement and the project pages on Adobe Labs.
