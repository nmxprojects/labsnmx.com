How much should an Indian startup spend on Meta ads? A budget guide with the maths

“Start with ₹500 a day” is the most common advice and usually the wrong number. Your budget should come from your cost per sale, not from a round figure. Here’s how to work it out.

By Neeraj, NMX Labs24 September 2026 · Updated 8 October 20265 min read
Listen to this articleSummarise with Google AI
Quick summary
  • Work out your maximum affordable cost per acquisition from margin and repeat orders before picking a budget.
  • An ad set needs roughly 50 optimisation events a week to leave learning: daily budget ≈ (50 × target CPA) ÷ 7.
  • If that’s out of reach, consolidate ad sets, optimise for an earlier event or raise order value.
  • Keep 20–30% for testing, scale winners gradually, and plan for GST and festive-season costs.

Founders ask us some version of the same question every week: how much should we put into Meta ads? The answer people usually get is a round number. The useful answer is a calculation, and you can do it on the back of an envelope.

This guide is general guidance from running campaigns, not financial advice. Platform rules and taxes change, so check Meta’s current documentation and your accountant for your specific situation.

Start from what a customer is worth

Before any ad spend, write down three numbers:

  1. Average order value (AOV): what a typical first order is worth.
  2. Gross margin: what you keep after product cost, shipping, packaging, payment fees and likely returns. For COD-heavy brands, include the cost of orders that are refused at the door.
  3. Repeat behaviour: roughly how many orders a customer places in a year.

From those you get your maximum affordable cost per acquisition (CPA). If your first order makes ₹600 in margin and you need to break even on the first order, your CPA ceiling is ₹600. If customers reliably reorder, you may choose to pay more for the first sale. That’s a business decision, not an ads setting.

The learning phase sets your minimum

Meta’s delivery system needs data to optimise. Meta’s guidance has long been that an ad set needs around 50 optimisation events (for example, purchases) within a week to exit the learning phase. Until then, results are less stable and usually more expensive.

“…the learning period stabilizes with a minimum of 50 events over a 7-day period.”

Meta for Developers, optimising an ad campaign

That gives a simple formula for the daily budget an ad set needs:

Daily budget ≈ (50 × target CPA) ÷ 7

Worked through for a few typical cases:

Target CPAWeekly spend for ~50 purchasesDaily budget per ad set
₹200₹10,000≈ ₹1,430
₹400₹20,000≈ ₹2,860
₹800₹40,000≈ ₹5,710

This is why “₹500 a day” disappoints so many brands with a ₹400 CPA. At that budget the ad set gets roughly nine purchases a week, never leaves learning, and the founder concludes Meta “doesn’t work”.

If the number is too big for you

That’s normal for an early brand. You have three honest options:

  • Consolidate. Run fewer ad sets so the budget isn’t split thin. One well-funded ad set learns faster than four starved ones.
  • Optimise for an earlier event. If you can’t afford 50 purchases a week, optimise for add-to-cart or initiate checkout, which happen more often, while you build data. Watch purchase cost separately, because the cheapest add-to-carts aren’t always the best buyers.
  • Raise the value of a conversion. Bundles, a higher-AOV hero offer or a better landing page all lower the effective CPA, which lowers the budget you need.

Split testing and scaling budgets

Once you’re spending steadily, separate money that proves things from money that earns. A rule of thumb we start from:

  • About 70–80% on proven ads and audiences (scaling).
  • About 20–30% on testing new creative concepts and offers.

Without a testing budget, your winners eventually fatigue and you have nothing ready to replace them. Our creative system post explains how we structure those tests.

Scale gradually

When something works, the temptation is to triple its budget overnight. Big jumps can push an ad set back into learning and spike costs. We increase budgets on winning ad sets in steady steps, give each change a few days, and duplicate into new campaigns when we want a bigger jump.

What about ROAS?

Return on ad spend (revenue ÷ ad spend) is the number most dashboards lead with, and it’s easy to misread. A 3× ROAS sounds healthy, but if your gross margin is 30%, every ₹100 of spend brings ₹300 of revenue and ₹90 of margin, so you’re losing ₹10 on each sale before overheads. Work out your break-even ROAS first: it’s 1 ÷ gross margin. At 30% margin that’s about 3.3×; at 60% margin it’s about 1.7×. Anything above break-even is profit on the first order; anything below needs repeat purchases to justify it.

Also remember that Meta’s reported ROAS is Meta’s view of attribution. Compare it with your own order data and the share of new versus returning customers before you scale on it.

Plan for India’s sale calendar

Costs aren’t flat across the year. Around Diwali and the big marketplace sale events, many brands raise budgets at the same time, and auction costs usually climb with them. If you sell gifts or festive products, that may still be your best month, so plan budget and creative for it weeks ahead. If you don’t, a quieter stretch can be a cheaper time to test new creative and build data, so you enter the next peak with proven ads.

Don’t forget the costs outside Ads Manager

  • GST. Indian advertisers are charged GST on Meta ad spend, which shows up on the invoice rather than inside your campaign budget. Plan cash flow for it, and talk to your accountant about claiming input credit if you’re registered.
  • Creative. Ads need a steady supply of new creative. Budget for it every month, not once.
  • Management. Whether in-house time or an agency fee, someone has to run and read the account.
  • Tracking. A correctly set-up pixel and Conversions API matter. If Meta can’t see your purchases, it can’t optimise for them, and every number above becomes guesswork.

A sensible first 30 days

  1. Calculate your CPA ceiling from margin.
  2. Check tracking: test a purchase and confirm Meta records it.
  3. Launch one campaign, one or two ad sets, three to five creatives built on different angles.
  4. Fund the ad set for learning using the formula, or optimise for an earlier event if you can’t.
  5. Read results after meaningful spend, keep winners, cut losers, iterate weekly.
