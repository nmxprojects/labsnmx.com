AI in your Meta ads: the “AI info” label, automatic enhancements and what the new terms mean

Meta now labels AI-made ads, its tools can change your creative after you upload it, and its updated commercial terms put more of the responsibility on you. A practical checklist.

By Anisha, NMX Labs5 November 20263 min read
Listen to this articleSummarise with Google AI
Quick summary
  • Meta applies an “AI info” label to ads made or significantly edited with its own AI tools, and detects some third-party AI through industry signals.
  • Ads showing a photorealistic AI-generated person get the label next to “Sponsored”.
  • Automatic creative enhancements can alter what people see; review those settings on every campaign.
  • Meta’s updated commercial terms (from 30 October 2026) place broad responsibility for AI output on the business.

AI is now part of most ad production, ours included. What changed over the past year is that it has become visible to the people seeing the ads, and the platform has become clearer that responsibility for what AI produces sits with the advertiser.

The “AI info” label

In early 2025 Meta said it would show “AI info” labels in the “About this ad” menu for ads created or significantly edited with its own generative AI tools, such as background generation, image generation and animation. It also said it would detect ads made or edited with third-party AI tools through industry-standard signals, the metadata that some tools attach to files.

“…automatically detecting ads created or edited using third-party AI tools…”

Meta Newsroom, Expanding GenAI Transparency for Meta’s Ads Products (updated June 2026)

One case is more prominent. When an ad includes a photorealistic AI-generated person, the label appears next to “Sponsored” in the feed itself, not hidden behind the menu.

Political and social-issue ads have separate, stricter disclosure rules.

Does the label hurt performance?

Meta hasn’t published evidence that it does, and we wouldn’t design around a guess. What the label does change is trust. A generated background behind a real product rarely bothers anyone. A generated “customer” giving a testimonial is a different matter, and one we won’t make, label or no label.

Automatic enhancements: check what people actually see

Advantage+ creative includes optional enhancements that can adjust your ad automatically: changing brightness and contrast, expanding images to fit placements, adding animation, generating background variations or rewriting text variations. Some help. Some produce something your brand team never approved.

In 2026 several advertisers publicly pulled ads after finding that automated edits had changed their creative in ways they hadn’t signed off. For a brand, that’s not a technical detail: a changed product colour, a cropped disclaimer or a rewritten claim is your ad.

Our rule: enhancements are off by default and switched on deliberately, one at a time, for ads where we’ve checked the result. We also preview every placement before launch and look at live ads on a phone after any account-level setting changes.

The updated commercial terms

Meta’s revised commercial terms took effect on 30 October 2026. They consolidate its AI advertising terms and, as reported in the trade press, place broad responsibility for the output of AI features on the business that uses them. If an AI tool inside Ads Manager produces a misleading claim or an image that infringes someone’s rights, the starting position is that it’s your ad.

If you run regulated products, licensed images or campaigns with health, finance or performance claims, have whoever handles compliance read the current terms rather than a summary.

A checklist for AI creative on Meta

  1. Keep the product real. Composite real packshots into generated scenes. Never let a model redraw your label or logo. Our AI product photography guide covers the workflow.
  2. No synthetic people pretending to be customers. If a person appears to use or recommend the product, they should be real and have agreed to it.
  3. Review enhancement settings on every campaign, including duplicated ones, which can carry settings over.
  4. Keep a simple record of which assets used AI and which tools, so you can answer a client, platform or regulator quickly.
  5. Check claims, prices and disclaimers in every text variation, including any the platform generates.
  6. Look at the live ad on a real phone in each placement, not just the preview.

The bigger picture

The direction is clear across platforms: more AI in ad production, more labelling of it, and more responsibility on the advertiser. None of that is a reason to avoid AI. It’s a reason to use it the way a careful studio uses any tool, with a person who checks the result before it goes out under your name. That’s the same principle behind our AI design workflow.
