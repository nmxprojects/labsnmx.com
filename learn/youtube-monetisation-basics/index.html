How YouTube creators get paid, and what changes on 1 February 2027

The YouTube Partner Programme’s entry bar for ad earnings doubles for new applicants on 1 February 2027. Here’s what the requirements are, what’s changing, and the income that doesn’t depend on them.

By Neeraj, NMX Labs27 October 20266 min read

Quick summary

  • Fan funding (memberships, Super Thanks and more) starts at 500 subscribers.
  • Ad revenue needs 1,000 subscribers plus 4,000 watch hours or 10 million Shorts views, until 31 January 2027.
  • From 1 February 2027, new applicants need 8,000 watch hours or 20 million Shorts views; channels already in are not affected.
  • Brand deals, affiliates and your own products often pay more than ads for small channels.

“How many subscribers do I need to earn?” is probably the question we’re asked most. The answer changed this year, so here is where things stand. Requirements can change again, so always confirm in the Earn section of YouTube Studio and on YouTube’s help pages.

The YouTube Partner Programme, in two levels

Level 1: fan funding

Channels can apply for fan-funding features such as channel memberships, Super Thanks, Super Chat and Super Stickers, and for YouTube Shopping, once they have:

  • 500 subscribers,
  • 3 public uploads in the last 90 days, and
  • either 3,000 public watch hours in the last 12 months or 3 million public Shorts views in the last 90 days.

Level 2: ad revenue and YouTube Premium

To earn a share of ads and YouTube Premium revenue, the bar today is 1,000 subscribers plus either 4,000 public watch hours in the last 12 months or 10 million public Shorts views in the last 90 days.

What changes on 1 February 2027

In August 2026, YouTube announced its first big change to these thresholds in years. From 1 February 2027, new applicants for ad and Premium revenue will need 1,000 subscribers plus either 8,000 qualified watch hours in the last 365 days or 20 million qualified Shorts views in the last 90 days. That’s double today’s bar. Channels already in the programme aren’t affected, and the fan-funding level stays as it is.

If your channel is close to the current bar, the deadline matters: apply as soon as you qualify. If you’re just starting, plan for the higher number, and lean on the income below in the meantime.

What counts

Only watch time from public long videos (and archived live streams) counts as qualified watch hours. Private, unlisted and deleted videos don’t count, nor do views that came from your own ad campaigns, nor watch time in the Shorts feed. You also need to follow YouTube’s monetisation policies (see our lesson on the rules), have no active Community Guidelines strikes, turn on 2-step verification and link an AdSense account. Reviews typically take about a month.

Money that doesn’t wait for the Partner Programme

  • Brand deals. Small channels with a clear niche often get paid collaborations before they qualify for ads. A focused audience of 3,000 people who all care about home baking can be worth more to the right brand than 50,000 random viewers. Always disclose them.
  • Affiliate links. Recommend tools you actually use through affiliate programmes, and say so.
  • Your own products or services. Courses, templates, consulting, a physical product. For many educational creators, YouTube is the shop window and this is the shop.
  • Selling your skill. The ability to script, shoot and edit videos is itself in demand from local businesses.

Paperwork

YouTube income is taxable in India like any other income, and brand payments often come with TDS. In AdSense, submit your tax information when asked; without it, Google can withhold a larger share of what you earn from viewers in the United States. A chartered accountant can tell you what applies to you; this isn’t tax advice.
