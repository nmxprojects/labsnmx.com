A logo that survives at 16 pixels

Your logo will spend most of its life tiny: a browser tab, an app icon, a profile picture in someone’s notifications. Design for that first and the big version takes care of itself.

By Anisha, NMX Labs20 November 20264 min read

Quick summary

  • Test every logo idea at 16, 32 and 64 pixels before you fall in love with it. Browser tabs and profile pictures are where people actually see it.
  • Plan a set, not one file: a full lockup, a short version or symbol, and a one-colour version.
  • Deliver SVG for the web and print, plus PNGs for social profiles, so nobody has to screenshot your logo.

Here’s an exercise that saves careers. Take your favourite logo concept, shrink it to the size of a browser tab icon, about 16 pixels, and look at it from across the room. Can you still tell what it is? Most first drafts turn into a smudge. That smudge is how a lot of people will meet your brand.

Where your logo really lives

  • Browser tabs and bookmarks: 16 to 32 pixels.
  • Profile pictures: a circle, often cropped, often smaller than your thumbnail.
  • App icons and notifications: a square that sits next to everyone else’s square.
  • The corner of a video or an ad: small, over a busy background.

Billboards are lovely. You will make very few of them.

Build a set, not a file

A working logo is a small family:

  1. The full lockup for your website header and decks.
  2. The short form, a symbol or a monogram, for small and square spaces.
  3. A one-colour version that works in black, in white and in your main colour. If it only works in a gradient, it doesn’t work.

The small-size checklist

  • Thick enough strokes. Hairlines vanish.
  • Few details. Every extra line is a future smudge.
  • A clear silhouette. Squint test: blur your eyes and it should still be recognisable.
  • Space around it. Decide the minimum clear space and stick to it.

Hand over the right files

SVG scales without losing quality, so it’s the master for the web and for print. Add PNGs with transparent backgrounds for social profiles, and a square version with padding for app icons and avatars. Name them so a stranger understands them. “logo-final-FINAL-2.png” is not a system.
