Shorts and long videos: how to make them help each other

Shorts bring new people; long videos keep them. How the two formats differ, how to connect them, and a simple weekly mix for a small channel.

By Neeraj, NMX Labs20 October 20265 min read

Quick summary

  • Shorts are vertical and can be up to 3 minutes; they’re for discovery.
  • Long videos build watch hours, trust and most of a channel’s income.
  • Shorts watch time doesn’t count towards the long-form watch-hours goal for monetisation.
  • Link each Short to a related long video, and remake ideas for the format instead of chopping clips.

Every small creator asks the same question sooner or later: should I make Shorts or long videos? The useful answer is both, for different jobs, connected on purpose.

What each format is good at

Shorts: discovery

Shorts are vertical (or square) videos, now up to 3 minutes long, shown in a swipeable feed to people who have never heard of you. They’re the fastest way to put your face and ideas in front of strangers. The trade-off: viewers swipe quickly, many don’t look at who made the Short, and a viral Short doesn’t guarantee anyone watches your next video.

Long videos: depth and trust

Long videos are found through search, recommendations and your subscribers. They’re where people get to know you, where most watch time comes from, and where most channels earn the bulk of their ad income and sponsorships. A viewer who watches 12 minutes of you explaining something is far more likely to come back than one who watched 20 seconds.

They count differently

For the YouTube Partner Programme, the two formats have separate routes. Long videos count through public watch hours over 12 months; Shorts count through Shorts views over 90 days. Watch time from the Shorts feed doesn’t count towards the long-form watch-hours goal. So 50 viral Shorts won’t add up to watch hours, and that’s fine, because they’re doing a different job.

Connect them

  • Link each Short to a related long video. YouTube lets you attach a related video to a Short, and viewers can tap through. Use it every time.
  • Make the Short a trailer, not a summary. Show the problem or the most surprising moment, and leave the full answer for the long video.
  • Use the same face and voice. Consistency helps the people who swipe past twice recognise you the third time.

Remake, don’t chop

Cutting random 60-second pieces out of a horizontal video rarely works. They start mid-sentence, the framing crops your head, and there’s no hook. Instead, pick one strong moment, give it a first line that works on its own, and reframe it vertically with large captions. Better still, film Shorts separately; a vertical take of your key point takes two extra minutes on shoot day.

A weekly mix for a small channel

  • One long video on a topic people search for or a question your audience keeps asking.
  • Two or three Shorts around it: the hook, a surprising tip, a mistake to avoid, each linked to the long video.
  • One look at the numbers at the end of the week (see the analytics lesson) to decide next week’s topic.

If you can only manage one format, choose the one you’ll make consistently for six months. A channel that posts one good long video a week for a year usually beats one that posts Shorts daily for a month and stops.
