You don’t need an iPhone: shoot your first video on the phone you already have

Most first videos fail on light, sound and nerves, not on the camera. Here’s how to get a clean, watchable first video out of a ₹12,000 Android phone, plus the three cheap things worth buying later.

By Anisha, NMX Labs1 September 20266 min read

Quick summary

  • Any phone from the last few years records 1080p video that is good enough for YouTube.
  • Use the back camera, lock focus and exposure, and face a window.
  • Sound matters more than picture: get the phone or a mic close to your mouth.
  • Spend money in this order: a ₹300–700 tripod, then a mic, then light. A new phone comes last.

The most common reason people give for not starting a channel is gear: “I’ll start when I get an iPhone.” We hear it from students, founders and even brand teams. The truth is less exciting and more useful. Almost any phone sold in India in the last four or five years records 1080p video that YouTube will happily show to millions of people. What separates a watchable first video from an unwatchable one is light, sound and how you start talking, and all three are free to fix.

What viewers actually notice

Watch a few videos from small channels that are growing. You’ll notice that very few are sharp, cinematic or colour graded. What they have in common is that you can hear the person clearly, you can see their face, and they get to the point. That’s the bar for your first video. Not perfect. Clear.

Set up the phone in five minutes

  1. Clean the lens. A pocket-smudged lens makes everything look foggy. Wipe it with a soft cloth.
  2. Use the back camera. It’s nearly always better than the selfie camera. Frame the shot with the front camera if you need to see yourself, then switch.
  3. Pick 1080p. 1080p at 30 frames per second is plenty. Choose 4K only if you have the storage and a computer that can edit it smoothly.
  4. Lock focus and exposure. On most Android phones and on iPhone, tap and hold on your face until the camera shows a lock. This stops the picture pumping brighter and darker as you move.
  5. Turn on Do Not Disturb. A WhatsApp call in the middle of a good take is the classic first-video disaster.
  6. Shoot horizontal for long videos, vertical for Shorts. Decide before you press record; cropping later throws away quality.

Keep it steady

A shaky handheld shot reads as amateur faster than anything else. If you don’t own a tripod yet, prop the phone against a stack of books at eye level. When you do buy something, a basic phone tripod with a clamp costs a few hundred rupees and fixes this for good. Put the lens at your eye height or slightly above. A camera looking up your nose is the second classic mistake.

Light: face the window

Daylight from a window is the best free light you’ll get. Sit facing it, or with it at an angle to one side, never behind you. Switch off the overhead tube light if it’s fighting the daylight and turning your skin two different colours. Filming at the same time of day keeps your videos looking consistent.

Sound: get closer

Your phone’s microphone is fine if it’s close to you and the room is quiet. It sounds hollow when it’s two metres away in a tiled room. So move the phone closer, switch off the fan and the cooler while you record, and film in a room with curtains, cushions or a bed. If your phone still has a headphone jack, or you have wired USB-C earphones with a mic, clip the mic part to your collar. It’s a free lapel mic.

What to buy, in order

  1. A tripod with a phone clamp, roughly ₹300 to ₹700.
  2. A clip-on microphone, wired or wireless, roughly ₹1,000 to ₹3,000 for a decent starter pair.
  3. A small LED light, only once you’re filming after dark regularly.

A new phone sits at the bottom of this list. Upgrade the camera when your audience tells you it’s the thing holding the videos back, not before.

Your homework

Record a two-minute video today, on the phone in your pocket, explaining one thing you know well. Don’t publish it. Watch it back with headphones and write down one fix for light, one for sound and one for how you opened. Then record it again. That second take is your first real video.
