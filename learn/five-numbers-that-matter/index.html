Learn / Meta ads for small budgets

Lesson 2 of 3

The five numbers that matter, and the vanity ones to ignore

Ads Manager shows you about two hundred columns. Five of them tell you almost everything, and they also tell you which part of your funnel is broken. Here they are, with one formula that ties them together.

By Neeraj, NMX Labs22 November 20265 min read

Quick summary

  • Watch CPM, link CTR, cost per click, conversion rate and cost per result (or ROAS). Together they show where a campaign breaks.
  • Cost per result is roughly CPM divided by 1,000, times CTR, times conversion rate, flipped. Improve any one and the result gets cheaper.
  • High CPM points at audience or ad quality, low CTR at the hook, good CTR with few sales at the landing page or offer.
  • Likes and reach alone are vanity metrics for a sales campaign.

Ads Manager is a cockpit with two hundred dials, and most people stare at the wrong ones. For a small account you need five, and the trick is reading them in order, like a funnel.

The five

  1. CPM: the cost of 1,000 impressions. What it costs to be seen.
  2. Link CTR: the share of people who saw the ad and clicked through. Whether the ad stopped them.
  3. CPC (link): what each click costs. CPM and CTR combined.
  4. Conversion rate: the share of clickers who bought or signed up. Whether your page and offer worked.
  5. Cost per result or ROAS: what a sale or lead costs, or how much revenue each rupee, pound or dollar brought back.

The one formula

Cost per result ≈ CPM ÷ (1,000 × CTR × conversion rate).

Example: a ₹200 CPM, 1% CTR and 2% conversion rate gives 200 ÷ (1,000 × 0.01 × 0.02) = ₹1,000 per sale. Double the CTR to 2% with a better hook and the same sale costs ₹500. That’s why creative work pays for itself.

Read it like a doctor

  • CPM unusually high? The audience is small or competitive, or the ad’s quality signals are weak. Try broader targeting or fresher creative.
  • CTR low? The first second isn’t working. Change the hook, the first frame or the headline, not the budget.
  • Good CTR, few sales? The ad is fine. The landing page, the price or the offer isn’t. Fix the page before you touch the ad.
  • Everything fine but frequency climbing? The same people are seeing it too often. New creative time.

The vanity numbers

Post likes, reach and video views feel good and pay nothing on a sales campaign. They’re useful for awareness campaigns or as hints, not as the score. If someone reports your sales campaign’s success in likes, ask politely for the cost per sale.
