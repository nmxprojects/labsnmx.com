Learn / Brand identity in a weekend

Lesson 1 of 3

Write the one sentence your brand stands on

Before colours, before a logo, before the mood board you’re itching to make: one sentence that says who you’re for and why you’re different. If a competitor could sign it, it isn’t finished.

By Anisha, NMX Labs19 November 20264 min read

Quick summary

  • Write your positioning as one sentence: who it’s for, what they need, what you are, how you’re different, and why anyone should believe it.
  • Test it: if a competitor could put their name on it, it’s too vague. Rewrite until they couldn’t.
  • Do this before any design work. Every visual decision later is easier when the sentence exists.

Most brand projects start in the wrong place: a Pinterest board, three logo ideas and a strong opinion about green. It’s more fun, I get it. But design without a point of view is decoration, and decoration is easy to copy.

The sentence

Fill in the blanks. Out loud. Ideally with someone who will laugh at you.

For [who exactly] who [need or frustration], [brand] is the [category] that [the difference] because [the proof].

A weak one: “For modern consumers who value quality, Brand X is the lifestyle brand that offers premium products because we care.” Every brand on earth could sign that, including the ones that don’t care.

A strong one: “For new parents who are tired of guessing, Brand X is the baby-food subscription that tells you exactly what your child needs each week, because a paediatric nutritionist plans every box.” You can design from that. You can write ads from that. You can say no to things because of that.

The competitor test

Cover your brand name and show the sentence to someone. Could it belong to your nearest competitor? If yes, sharpen one of three things:

  • The who. “Everyone” isn’t an audience. Narrow is brave, and it’s also cheaper to advertise to.
  • The difference. One thing, not five. The one you’d still say if you only had a second.
  • The proof. The reason to believe. A process, a person, a number, a guarantee. Something checkable.

What to do with it

Put it at the top of your brand guidelines, your agency brief and the prompt you give AI tools. When someone suggests a new product, a new colour or a new campaign, hold it up against the sentence. If it doesn’t fit, it’s probably a different brand.

Homework: write three versions, pick the one that’s slightly uncomfortable, and sleep on it. The comfortable ones are usually the vague ones.
